Holiday-1. <Tell me about the holidays in your country. What kinds of traditional foods do they enjoy on that day? Which things have changed over time?>


Ok Eva, one of the most meaningful holidays in Korea is Seollal, the Lunar New Year.
[kick]


On that day, we dress up in traditional clothes, hanbok and enjoy all kinds of delicious food. But the most iconic dish is definitely tteokguk, a rice cake soup. Sliced rice cakes float in a warm broth, topped with a bit of egg garnish and shredded seaweed. There's actually a fun saying that eating a bowl of tteokguk makes you a year older, so little kids often go for a second bowl!
[kick] makes you a year older; go for a second bowl


But traditionally, it was mostly the women in the family who did all the cooking, and honestly, cooking for a big family isn't a joke. So these days, men pitch in a lot more with the kitchen duties too. And some families take it a step further, skipping the home-cooked meal altogether and having breakfast at a luxury hotel instead. Others just pack their bags and head overseas for the holiday.
